# Phone UI audit of Direction and Settings

An isolated SillyTavern instance on ports 8331 and 8332 served the working extension and a mock completion API. Chromium used a 390 × 820 viewport. The disposable chat with Seraphina contained a user message about the harbor and a model reply. Extension edits were made via visible controls, including native selections, expanded groups, named checkboxes and Save. Accessibility observations came from Chromium’s accessibility tree. The mock establishes which choices and text reach a request and what persists, but cannot judge a production model’s prose.

- Random Instructions. Exercised the nine actor and eight target choices individually, the exclusive model actor, two scene actors with two targets, and five singleton combinations. Character/character, new/new, another named character/another named character, surroundings/new and male/male showed distinct pairing guidance. With the model actor selected, the writing prompt changed to “What should the model do?” and character speech triggered a warning to choose System note. Choosing a scene actor displaced the model. The saved two-actor/two-target pools survived reopening. With four requested options the review displayed two new variants, identified by their individual actor/target draws; two returned mock strings had already been saved in the previous generation. All four Sent as options changed help and/or preview framing. All three allowed-trigger modes and placement choices were exercised. Word and pattern triggers showed the corresponding text field and offered the host chat scan, latest user message and previous model reply. Selecting character-description placement removed the chat depth field.
- Random Story Events. Created a set and added two Situation items. Selected each of the three “How it changes” modes. Choosing a fixed bell item put the exact item in the preview. Clicked every event-origin checkbox in the AI writer, selected only Another character and The surroundings, generated four suggestions, accepted the default ticked suggestions, and saved. The saved three generated items had origin world, other and world; the rejected suggestion was absent.
- Random Writing Style. Created a set, exercised all three hold modes, selected “plain words and brisk rhythm”, selected Out-of-character note, unlocked Point of view and tense, turned it on and selected “first person, present tense”. Both exact choices appeared in the OOC preview and survived Save.
- Settings. Selected all five character pronoun presets. The four named presets exposed no custom boxes. “Other (type your own)” exposed five individually named fields and accepted “ze” in the subject field. Switching reply checking off hid its agency control and switching it on restored all three options. Exercised all three Under each reply choices and verified their different explanations of visibility and storage.

## Usability findings from the first pass

1. The collapsed actor and target pickers had indistinguishable names when their values were the same. “A named character who is present, other than you” omitted that the main character was excluded. These findings were sent to the implementer.
2. In an open populated category, each existing item's Weight input had no accessible name, Tags was read as “none” and the text editor opened unnamed. These controls were missed by the initial check of unpopulated page landing screens.
3. In Prose style, changing the minimum hold from four to twelve left the maximum showing eight after Tab. Leaving and returning to the hold mode re-rendered the actual stored 12/12. The visible bounds could therefore misrepresent the setting.

The check did not cross every actor/target permutation, interact with all settings variants, exercise group chat, use a physical phone or mobile screen reader, or test model obedience. Actual event/style output was assessed in preview. The interface ran against a mock.

## Retest after the fixes

Copied the updated extension to the isolated installation, reloaded Chromium and reopened the saved sets in the same populated chat. All three findings above passed their retests.

- Chromium’s accessibility tree now identifies the collapsed controls as separate DisclosureTriangle nodes named “Actor: Another named character in the scene (excluding {{char}} and {{user}})” and “Target or subject: Another named character in the scene (excluding {{char}} and {{user}})”. Enter opened both controls. Selecting the option on both sides showed the updated two-person-scene warning.
- The first Prose style row exposed “Weight for short sentences and concrete nouns”, “Tags for short sentences and concrete nouns” and “Item text for Prose style, item 1”. The Situation row exposed the equivalent names for the harbor bell. Entered weight 3 and tag harbor by accessible name, saved and confirmed both values in the persisted lorebook.
- Raising the minimum to 18 immediately showed 18/18. Entering 6 in the maximum and pressing Tab also showed 18/18. Both values were 18 in the saved style set. The editor had client and scroll widths of 368 px at the 390 px viewport. No browser page exceptions were collected.
